About — Vol. IV, Issue 04 Brooklyn, NY · Founded 2018 Issue dated May 2026

We started this because the night dispatcher kept losing sleep.

Tsatsral Khaltar Founder & CEO Photographs by
Felix Okonkwo

Tsatsral Limo started in 2018 with three Cadillacs, a flip phone, and a whiteboard in a rented bay on Atlantic Avenue. The dispatcher was Tsatsral's father — and on the third night of the third month, he called her at 4 a.m. to ask which car was supposed to be at JFK.

So she built the dispatch board they wished they'd had. Then a reservations queue. Then a portal so the corporate accounts would stop calling. Six years later, two hundred operators across fourteen cities run on the same software, and her father sleeps through the night.

This is, in the literal sense, the operating system we needed to keep our own family business from grinding itself into the dashboard.

A note from the founder

Software shouldn't be the second-hardest part of running a fleet.

I grew up in cars. My father drove for hire in Ulaanbaatar, then in Queens, then in Brooklyn — and the only thing he ever complained about was the paperwork. By the time we owned three vehicles, there were four spreadsheets, two binders, and a dispatch board made of magnets.

The software available to us wanted us to either hire a full-time admin or to keep using the magnets. Neither answer was acceptable to a family that worked 18-hour days and still felt like the night was running them.

Tsatsral is the answer I would have wanted in 2017 — and the team that built it knows what it's like to have to ask the chauffeur to use his own phone because the company tablet died.

— Tsatsral Khaltar
House rules

Three things we will not negotiate.

A short list of what we believe — written so plainly that an operator three time zones away can hold us to it without a meeting.

01 — One ledger

Reservations and dispatch read from the same row.

If two screens disagree about a trip, one of them is lying. We picked a side: the trip ledger is the truth, and every product is just a different lens on it.

02 — Honest pricing

The price you quote is the price you charge.

No surge. No "the meter said." Long-distance is fixed-quoted by the mile, airport runs include 15 minutes free, and gratuity is a number — not a guilt-trip slider.

03 — Boring at scale

The 4 a.m. trip works the same way the noon trip does.

Drama is a sign your software is wrong. We treat boring as a feature — the same workflow at 04:55 as at 14:30, the same dispatch UI on the third trip as on the three-thousandth.

Notebook entries · 2018–2026

Eight years of trips, mistakes, and the occasional fix.

2018

Three cars, one whiteboard.

Tsatsral and her father open the bay on Atlantic Ave. Annual revenue: $184,000. Number of times she's woken up at 4 a.m. by the JFK call: 41.

The whiteboard collapses on a Friday in October. We start dispatching from a shared Google Sheet. The Sheet outgrows itself by Christmas.

2020

The pandemic year.

Airport revenue collapses 78%. We pivot to long-distance and grocery runs for elderly clients in Brooklyn — and stay open.

Customer #214 starts using us monthly for a Boston commute. She's now Customer #214 with 184 trips and counting.

2022

First version of the dispatch board.

Built on weekends by a chauffeur named Markus who used to write Java. Six other operators ask if they can buy it. We start charging.

By the end of the year there are 14 fleets on the platform. Two of them are bigger than ours.

2024

Customer portal launches.

White-labelled. A travel desk at a Greenwich hedge fund is the first to use it. They book 220 trips in the first month — and never call us once.

We hire two full-time engineers. The chauffeur-engineer remains a chauffeur on weekends because he likes it.

2026

Two hundred operators, fourteen cities.

1,200 rides dispatched daily across the platform. The whiteboard is in the office — framed.

Tsatsral's father still rides shotgun on the JFK 04:55 once a week. He says it's for quality control. We know it's because he likes it.

Manifesto

Six things we'll be boring about for the next ten years.

  1. One trip ledger. We will not ship a feature that requires reconciling two databases. The trip is the row. Everyone reads from it.
  2. The chauffeur sees only their trips. The driver app shows the work. It does not show the back-office. It does not show prices. It does not show other chauffeurs' performance. It is a tool, not a panopticon.
  3. Customers own their data, full stop. Export to CSV is a P0. Erase on request is a P0. We will not sell, mine, or "anonymize-and-share" your rider data — ever.
  4. Pricing is a number, not a feeling. Quote = charge. Surcharges are listed in the quote. Tips are a percentage the rider picks, not a slider with a pre-checked default we hope they don't notice.
  5. We answer the phone. The founder's number is in the welcome email. It is not a chatbot. It is not a rota. If you call between 06:00 and 22:00 ET, a human picks up — usually within four rings.
  6. We will not chase a unicorn. We are a software company that started as a limo company. We intend to remain useful to a 3-vehicle fleet on day 4,000.
